2. IAS 29 and the correct quarter

Under IAS 29 each report is stated in the purchasing power of its own date. Companies publish year-to-date figures; subtracting 6M from 9M mixes two different liras. KAPSAM restates the prior period to current lira with the CPI ratio:

Quarter(t) = YTD(t) − YTD(t−1) × CPI(t) / CPI(t−1)

The unadjusted-difference error is the deviation of naive subtraction from the correct quarter. Banks and insurers do not apply IAS 29; their quarters are derived nominally and real trends are shown by restating with CPI.

3. Core earnings and earnings quality

Core net income = net income − net monetary position gain. Monetary gains arise from inflation eroding debt and are not cash. Trailing-twelve-month figures = YTD + prior year − prior-year same period, all in current lira. Nine-month figures are never annualised automatically.

4. DCF valuation

For industrial companies real free cash flow to the firm is discounted: a 10-year explicit period plus terminal value. Revenue is TTM (IAS 29-adjusted), operating margin is the TTM core operating margin (excluding monetary gains), reinvestment follows the sales-to-capital ratio, tax 25%, real WACC 10%, terminal real growth 2%. Results come with a sensitivity table (WACC × growth) and the market-implied growth and margin (reverse DCF); a formula-driven Excel model can be downloaded for each company. Banks and insurers use P/B, P/E and return on equity instead.

DCF assumptions are automatic and identical for all companies; they exclude company-specific information (new investments, concession terms, affiliates, one-offs). Values are highly sensitive to assumptions; test them in the Excel model.

5. Risk

Daily log returns over the last 252 trading days. Annualised volatility, downside volatility, maximum drawdown, 95% VaR and expected shortfall, beta to BIST 100 and idiosyncratic risk. Correlation and covariance use pairwise complete observations (at least 20 common days); the large matrix uses Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage (constant-correlation target) and a positive semi-definite projection. Fewer than 120 common days is flagged as low reliability.

6. Agenda

Twelve global/local indicators (BIST 100, USD/TRY, VIX, S&P 500, Brent, gold, US 10-year yield…) and news headlines. VIX above 25 or a monthly USD/TRY rise above 5% adds one point to the equity risk premium in the DCF discount rate.

7. Known limitations

  • Free data sources are delayed and occasionally incomplete; missing items are filled from a backup source and the rest are shown as "no data".
  • TTM figures may not be computable for recent IPOs.
  • Company counts differ by page (companies with statements, stocks with price series, the full universe).
  • News tone is automatic and crude; read the source.
  • This site is the output of a research tool; it contains no buy/sell calls, price targets or investment advice.
